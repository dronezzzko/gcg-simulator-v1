# Effect templates, DSL primitive inventory and work packages

Research input for the effect DSL, the text compiler (`gcg_sim.effects`) and the per-set card-binding
work. Everything below the prose is regenerated by `uv run python scripts/cluster_effects.py`
from the pinned gcg-api snapshot (commit `39c85b5`, dataset `28-ad9da1b…`, `cards.ndjson`
sha256 `9a5517ed…`). Rule ids refer to the Comprehensive Rules Ver. 1.9.0 (Sep 11, 2026).

Machine-readable outputs:

- `docs/research/effect_templates.json`: normalization log, templates (pattern, regex, slots,
  frequency, card numbers, examples, observed slot values), compositional `{SEL}` templates,
  selector phrases, irregular sentences, the primitive inventory, and a per-card parse tree
  (`cards.<number>.abilities[].sentences[].segments[]` with template ids). Per-set agents should
  start from the parse tree.
- `docs/research/work_packages.json`: 19 packages that partition all 1,148 card numbers.

## Summary

At this snapshot there are 1,148 card numbers (1,912 printings). 308 are vanilla after
normalization (125 Units, 20 Unit tokens, and every Resource, EX Base and EX Resource, whose
text is only rule 2-11-4 reminder text). That leaves 840 non-vanilla cards with 1,909 sentences
plus 191 structural lines (keyword lines and 【Pilot】[Name] lines).

| layer | templates (used by 2+ cards) | sentences templated | non-vanilla cards fully templated | cards with irregular sentences |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| flat: the typed slots listed in the brief | 648 (233) | 1,519 / 1,909 (79.6%) | 494 / 840 (58.8%) | 346 (390 sentences) |
| compositional: flat plus a `{SEL}` selector sub-grammar | 410 (185) | 1,700 / 1,909 (89.1%) | 651 / 840 (77.5%) | 189 (209 sentences) |

- **The effect language is small.** The 9 flat templates used by 50 or more cards already
  cover 530 sentences on their own (28%). The dominant shape is "Choose <selector>. <verb>
  it/them." (`C001 Choose {SEL}.` appears on 363 cards). The second is a prefix chain before a
  core, such as
  `During your turn, when this Unit destroys an enemy Unit with battle damage, draw 1.`
- **Selectors cause most of the flat irregulars.** Of the 390 flat-irregular sentences, 181
  compile once noun phrases go through the selector grammar (section 6.2). The other 209, on
  189 cards, need a per-card binding or a small dedicated primitive. They are listed in
  section 9 with `per-card binding = yes`.
- **Cost of the long tail.** With `{SEL}`, templates used by 3 or more cards (133) compile
  581 cards fully. Templates used by 2 or more (185) compile 651. The last 189 cards would
  need 225 single-use templates, one or two per card, so per-card bindings are the cheaper
  route for them.

## 1. Inputs and method

1. **Canonical printing.** For each `card_number`, the printing with `product_id == card_number` is
   used. Three card numbers have no such printing (`R-001`, `EXB-001`, `EXR-001`); for these the
   lowest `_pN` printing is used. They are rules-defined vanilla cards. 35 card numbers have
   printings with different raw text. Only 11 of them still differ after normalization
   (section 7). The canonical printing has the current wording for every erratum that gcg-api
   ships; the 2026-09-04 ST12-001 erratum is applied through `overrides.json` instead.
2. **Normalization** (section 2): rules N01–N09 are applied in order. The same function is used
   for every printing, so divergent printings are compared on normalized text.
3. **Parsing.** The normalized text is split on newlines. A line beginning with `■` is an option
   or granted-effect body and attaches to the previous line. Each line becomes one ability:
   - a leading chain of `【…】` markers (a `/` between markers means alternatives, as in
     `【Main】/【Action】` and `【Deploy】/【Attack】`), then
   - an optional activation cost (for `【Activate･…】` only: the text before `：`, split on `, `), then
   - a body.

   A body that is only `<Keyword n>` is a structural keyword line. `【Pilot】[Name]` is a
   structural pilot-name line.
4. **Sentences and segments.** A body is split into sentences at `.` followed by a space and a
   capital letter, `"`, `【`, `<`, `[`, `(` or `■`. `Lv.`/`Lv. is` never splits. Each sentence
   is then decomposed in this order:
   - a connector: `Then,` (rule 5-20-2) or `If you do,` (rule 5-20-1);
   - a trailing ` instead.`, recorded as a flag (the substitution wording of rule 10-1-9);
   - up to six leading prefix segments peeled off before the core:
     - scope: `During your turn/your opponent's turn/this turn/this battle/a turn where …,` and
       `On the turn this Unit is deployed,`;
     - trigger: `When …,`, `At the start/end of …,`, `After activating …,`;
     - condition: `If …,`, `While …,`.

   The remainder is the core, with its first letter capitalized. Prefixes never contain a
   comma, so this is how a regex compiler should peel them as well.
5. **Templates.** Every segment is tokenized left to right. Typed slots are replaced in the
   priority order `TOKEN, NAME, KW, TIMING, TRAIT, COLOR, CMP, RES, TYPE, ZONE, N` (the
   `slot_types` in the JSON). Segments with the same `(role, pattern)` form one template.
   Frequency is the number of distinct card numbers. A template is *regular* when at least
   2 cards use it. A sentence is *templated* when all its segments are regular, and a card is
   *fully templated* when all its sentences and cost clauses are. The script checks that every
   template regex round-trips all of its member texts.
6. **Compositional layer.** The selector grammar (section 6.2) rewrites noun phrases in flat
   patterns to `{SEL}` and the templates are clustered again. This layer is a heuristic
   estimate of what a compiler with a selector sub-parser achieves. The flat layer is the one
   defined by the brief.

## 2. Normalization

Notes:

- **N07 strips explanatory parentheticals only.** Rule 2-11-4 says parentheticals are
  explanatory and have no game effect. Three stripped notes (`(Don't treat it as a Pilot.)`
  GD05-089, `(This card that is being activated is not counted.)` ST14-014,
  `(You choose first, then your opponent.)` ST12-013) spell out resolution details. The
  bindings for those cards must still honour them.
- **N06 is a single data quirk.** GD02-053 prints `[Suppression]`. The gcg-api
  `keyword_effects` field for that card does not list Suppression.
- **Do not use `timing_markers` or `keyword_effects` as ability structure.** These gcg-api
  fields collect every marker and keyword that is mentioned anywhere in the text:
  - GD04-066 lists `Main`/`Action` because it says "a Command's 【Main】/【Action】 effect";
  - GD01-089 lists `Repair` because it says "while this Unit has `<Repair>`";
  - GD04-067 lists all seven keywords.

  Parse the text instead.

## 3. Ability kinds and marker chains

- `triggered` means the timing comes from a marker (【Burst】, 【Deploy】, 【Attack】,
  【Destroyed】, 【When Paired】, 【When Linked】).
- `triggered_text` means an untagged "When …/At the end of …" sentence (rule 10-1-6-1).
- `constant` means untagged static text (rule 10-1-5).
- `option` means a `■` body under "choose 1 of the following effects" or under Development.
- `cost_modifier` and `play_modifier` are hand-zone rules: "this card in your hand gets cost
  -N" and "When playing this card …, play it as if it has N Lv. and cost".
- `alias` means "This card's name is also treated as [X]".

## 6. Recommendations for the DSL and the compiler

### 6.1 Ability model

1. **Ability** = `host` + `timing` + `qualifiers` + `cost` + `body`, where:
   - `host` is the card itself, or the paired Unit;
   - `timing` is one of: keyword, constant, triggered(event), activated(Main|Action),
     command(Main|Action|Main/Action), burst;
   - `qualifiers` are `during_pair(pilot_filter?)`, `during_link` and `once_per_turn`;
   - `cost` is a list of cost atoms;
   - `body` is a list of steps.
2. **Pilot cards (rule 3-3-9).** Every non-【Burst】 ability on a Pilot card is hosted by the
   paired Unit, and "this Unit"/"this is a (X) Unit"/"it" refer to that Unit. The gcg-api text
   does not say which lines are printed above the card name, so this is inferred from rule
   3-3-9-1 ("Typically 【Burst】 effects appear here"). Treat 【Burst】 and the name alias
   (GD02-098, ST12-011, ST12-012) as belonging to the Pilot card itself, in every zone.
   GD05-089's 【Burst】 "deploy it as an (AP3･HP3) Unit instead" is a Pilot-card ability that
   turns the card into a Unit.
3. **Command 【Pilot】[Name] (68 cards, rule 3-4-6).** The Command can be played as a Pilot.
   Take its name from the bracket, AP/HP from the `ap_raw`/`hp_raw` fields (`+1`/`+0`) and
   traits from `traits`. GD05-112/113/121/122 and GD04-021 pair a Command from the trash, so
   the pairing action must accept any card with a 【Pilot】 line.
4. **Steps and connectors.**
   - A body is a sequence of steps. `Then,` continues even when the previous step did nothing
     (rule 5-20-2). `If you do,` is gated on the previous step having resolved (rule 5-20-1).
     Model "resolved" as a boolean returned by every step.
   - `You may X. If you do, Y.` is the universal optional-cost idiom (65 cards use `If you do`).
     It gates on discard, exiling from trash (including Development), resting or destroying
     your own Unit, damaging your own Unit, paying ①, or revealing from hand.
   - Commands whose first step chooses targets cannot be played without a legal target
     (rules 10-1-8-1-1 and 10-1-8-1-2). The compiler must tag each step with
     "requires target", so the engine can compute legal plays.
5. **Target bindings.**
   - A `Choose(selector, count, optional)` step binds the chosen cards to `it`/`them`. 530
     cards back-reference with it/them/its/they.
   - Other references to model: `this Unit`/`this Base`/`this card` (self); `that Unit`,
     `the enemy Unit` and `the attacking Unit` (the event subject); `the card paired with this
     Unit` (11 cards).
   - Count semantics: `Choose N` means up to N, performed as far as possible (rule 10-1-3), and
     choosing only happens among public cards (rule 10-2-2-1). `Choose N to M` is a range
     (19 cards). A selector phrase starting with `all` needs no choice.
   - Player choices are needed for "All enemy players each choose 1 of their …",
     "You and that player each choose 1 of your own Units" and "That player chooses 2 Unit
     cards from their trash".
6. **`instead` (18 cards).**
   - 12 uses are "If <cond>, <same verb with a different parameter or selector> instead"
     (GD01-122, GD03-104, GD03-109, GD03-114, GD03-115, GD04-039, GD04-106, GD04-108,
     ST02-016, ST07-009, ST08-013, ST14-014). Compile these as a conditional override of the
     previous step's parameters, not as a rule-10-1-9 substitution.
   - The true substitution effects (rule 10-1-9) are:
     - rest substitution: GD03-079, GD05-124;
     - battle-damage redirect: GD04-087, GD04-095;
     - "add to hand instead of returning": ST12-012;
     - deploy as a Unit instead: GD05-089.

### 6.3 Triggers and the event log

The engine needs a typed event stream, and a per-turn log for "this turn" history conditions.

- **Events from markers:** Deployed (incl. `from trash`), AttackDeclared, Destroyed (with
  cause battle/effect, destroying player, destroying card's traits), Paired and Linked,
  ShieldDestroyed/Burst.
- **Events from text** (the `trigger.*` rows):
  - destroying: DestroyedEnemyUnitWithBattleDamage, DestroyedShieldAreaCard (battle or any
    damage), DestroyedEnemyCard;
  - damage: BattleDamageDealtToUnit, DamageReceived (battle/effect, from an enemy, or from
    a Command);
  - state changes: APReducedByEnemyEffect, HPRecovered, RestedByEffect (and whose effect),
    SetActiveByEffect;
  - resources and costs: EXResourcePlaced, EXResourceExiled, CostPaidForUnitEffect (amount);
  - cards played: CommandPlayedUsingEXResource, CommandEffectActivated (trait);
  - combat: AttackDeclared by another Unit, Blocked-by;
  - other: SupportUsed, DrawByEffect, EndOfTurn, StartOfOpponentTurn;
  - play-time hooks: WhenPlayingThisCard (alternative cost, modal choice) and
    AfterThisCommandMain.
- **Replacement ("would") hooks:** WouldReceiveBattleDamage (ST14-006) and WouldRest
  (GD03-079, GD05-124).
- **Delayed triggers created by effects.** "During this turn, when it/they …" appears on
  GD04-002, GD04-035, GD04-115, GD05-002, GD05-018, GD05-094, GD05-125, ST11-006, ST11-012 and
  GD03-120. Granted abilities are GD05-104 and ST12-014. Model this as
  `CreateTrigger(event, filter bound to the chosen target, duration, steps)`.
- **Reflexive trigger.** GD03-001 "When this effect destroys an enemy Unit, draw 1." needs
  step results that carry what happened: destroyed cards, number rested, cards placed,
  cards drawn and discarded. EB01-003, EB01-050, GD03-092, GD03-094, GD04-116, GD05-052,
  GD05-097, ST07-001 and ST10-008 use the same data.

### 6.4 Conditions, costs, durations and continuous effects

- **Conditions** are boolean expressions over:
  - `count(selector) cmp N`, `exists`, `none`;
  - trash counts (filtered, including name and unique-name counts);
  - player Lv, turn owner, hand size, shield count;
  - self state: damaged, rested, AP/HP, has keyword, colour, trait, is Link, unpaired;
  - attack context (attacking the player / a Unit / a damaged Unit; battling a Unit with a
    property);
  - EX Resource state: none left, the opponent has one, one was used to play this card;
  - deploy source, destroyed-how;
  - turn-history flags and effect results (the `condition.*` rows).
- **Costs:** ①–④ resource payment; rest this Unit/Base; destroy this Unit; rest N (filtered)
  other Units; exile N (filtered) cards from trash; discard a filtered card; destroy a friendly
  token; return this Unit to the deck bottom; `<Support n>`. Development n (rule 13-1-8) is
  optional exile of n (G Generation) cards followed by the `■` effect.
- **Durations:** this turn, this battle, while <cond> (re-evaluated continuously,
  rule 10-1-5-3), during your/opponent's turn, until the start phase of the opponent's next
  turn (freeze), the next damage (one-shot), on the deploy turn, during pair/link.
- **Continuous effects:**
  - AP/HP modifiers, fixed or scaled by a count ("for each", "equal to the number of");
  - keyword grants, which add up for Repair/Breach/Support (rules 13-1-1-2, 13-1-2-5,
    13-1-3-2), plus the copy-keywords effect on GD04-067;
  - trait grants;
  - restrictions: can't attack, can't choose the player, can't activate `<Blocker>`, can't be
    set active, can't be paired, can't be attack target, must be attack target if possible,
    damage immunity by source filter, can't be destroyed by enemy effects, AP can't be
    reduced;
  - attack permissions: may attack active Units matching a filter; may attack on the deploy
    turn;
  - hand/trash cost and Lv modifiers (fixed or per count) and "deployed rested" statics.
  Keep a layer order for AP. Rule 10-1-5-6 says "can't" wins over "can".

### 6.5 1v1 scope and multiplayer wording

15 cards (mostly EB01) use multiplayer wording: "each enemy player", "2 or more enemy players",
"number of enemy players", "belonging to another player", "enemy player with the most Units".
Multiplayer is out of scope (SPEC: rules section 12 is N/A). In 1v1, compile these as follows:

- "number of enemy players" is 1;
- "2 or more enemy players" is false: EB01-040, EB01-044, EB01-055 and EB01-058 then do
  nothing;
- "each enemy player" / "another player" is the opponent;
- "the same enemy player or enemy team" (ST10-001) is the opponent.

Record these as explicit assumptions.

### 6.6 Compiler recommendations

1. Implement normalization N01–N09 exactly and parse with the same steps as section 1. The JSON
   parse tree is the reference output.
2. Match the core against the template table. The `regex` of every flat template is emitted.
   Try the more specific templates first, and run selector phrases through the `{SEL}`
   sub-grammar. The `compositional_template` field shows which flat templates merge.
3. Keep a per-card binding module for everything in `irregular_compositional`, which is 209
   sentences on 189 cards at this snapshot. Many of these are singletons of an ordinary
   primitive. Examples: "Place 3 EX Resources." (plural), "This Unit gets HP+1.", "Exile it
   from the game.". A per-set agent may choose to widen a template instead.
4. The compiler must fail loudly on unmatched text. The card-coverage test (SPEC criterion 5)
   should assert that every non-vanilla card compiles or has a binding.
5. Golden tests: render the compiled AST back to canonical text and compare it with the
   normalized text. Use the `examples` of each template as the first test vectors.
6. `effect_templates.json` records its source sha256. The refresh skill can rerun this script
   and diff `templates`/`irregular` to find new wording in a new set.

- **Printings whose normalized text differs from the canonical one:**

## 8. Work packages

- **Partition.** `work_packages.json` partitions every card number exactly once. The script
  verifies this and fails otherwise.
- **Split points.** GD01–GD05 and EB01 are split into two contiguous card-number ranges. The
  split point is chosen so that the two halves have nearly equal effort. Starter decks are
  grouped into packages of 42–64 cards. Tokens, EX cards and Resources each form their own
  package.
- **Effort model.** effort points = 0.5 per non-vanilla card + 0.5 per templated sentence +
  1.5 per sentence that needs the selector grammar + 3 per sentence that stays irregular.
  Sizes: XS < 15, S < 60, M < 110, L < 160, XL otherwise.
- **Binding lists.** Each package lists `irregular_card_numbers` (flat) and
  `per_card_binding_card_numbers` (compositional).
