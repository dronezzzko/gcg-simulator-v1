## Deck sanity runs (no AI tuning)

Each deck was played against the whole panel with the same agent in both seats, 40 games per
pair (10 mirrored blocks, `--choose-first seat0`), master seed 7, no `--role`. Seed 7 lies
outside every A14 range, so no role's seeds were used and nothing entered the seed ledger. A
deck's wins are its wins from either seat; no game was drawn or unfinished.

The new decks span 0.36 to 0.67 under `ref:fast`, the same range as the example decks (0.37 to
0.65): none is degenerate. The most lopsided pairs under `ref:fast` are `blue-green-ramp` vs
`purple-blue-swarm` and `purple-white-tekkadan` vs `purple-blue-swarm` (5/40 each for the first
deck). These are measurements of the decks under one agent, not strength claims for any agent.

The ramp deck went through four lists, each scored under `ref:fast` against the seven other
decks (seed 7). The first two, built around the ramp Commands First Contact and AGE Device (the
second with blue Blockers added), scored 66/280 = 0.236 and 67/280 = 0.239. On the second,
`gcg-sim benchmark --ai-preset fast` against `red-green-zeon` (20 BO1 games, seed 3) showed
First Contact played in 1 of the 11 games where it was drawn and 58% of the drawn AGE-1 Titus
(Lv.7) copies never played. The third and fourth lists replace those Commands with Units that
place EX Resources (G-Self, Nu Gundam) and with interaction (Fierce Enemy Assault, Signs of a
Revolution, Penelope (Middle Form)); they scored 99/280 = 0.354 and 97/280 = 0.346. The fourth is
the committed list (0.361 in the panel run above). A green-red ramp variant (red damage Commands and GQuuuuuuX
GD03-034 instead of the blue cards), tried in the scratch area and not committed, scored 75/200 =
0.375 against `blue-white-federation`, `purple-white-tekkadan`, `red-green-zeon`,
`purple-blue-swarm` and `red-purple-control`; the final blue-green list scored 65/200 = 0.325
against the same five in the panel run above. The difference (two-proportion z about 1.05) is
not significant at this size, so the deck kept blue-green, the colour pair no other panel deck
uses.
